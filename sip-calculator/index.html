What the calculator answers

It estimates a future corpus from monthly SIP amount, investment duration, annual return assumption and optional yearly step-up. It is useful for scenario planning, not return prediction.

What it deliberately does not claim

The selected return is not a forecast of a particular mutual fund. The calculator does not know future market returns, inflation, taxes, expense ratios or future changes in your ability to invest.

PlanSIP's two-view approach

Use the assumption calculator to plan. Then use Historical SIP Replay to see what selected funds actually did over an available historical period. Keeping those views separate avoids presenting history as a promise.

Better planning habit

Run at least three assumptions. If the target is missed at a conservative rate, consider increasing contribution, extending time, reducing the target or combining those changes rather than simply raising the assumed return.

Example: ₹15,000 per month for 10 years

Total contributions are ₹18 lakh before any return. A future-value estimate above ₹18 lakh comes from the chosen return assumption. If a 10% yearly step-up is enabled, contributions themselves also rise each year, so compare both invested amount and estimated gain.

How PlanSIP keeps this transparent

Inputs, assumptions and limitations are shown rather than hidden behind a single recommendation. Historical data and estimated future returns are treated differently. A screen or score is a research aid, not a buy signal.
