How a SIP Calculator Works: Formula, Assumptions and Reality Checks

A SIP calculator can answer a mathematical question precisely while still giving a financially misleading impression if the assumptions are unrealistic. The key is to separate contribution math from market uncertainty.

Written for: Indian mutual fund beginners   •   Last reviewed: 18 August 2026

Inputs that drive the result

Monthly contribution, number of months, assumed return and contribution timing drive a standard future-value estimate. Step-up calculators also increase the contribution at chosen intervals.

Contribution sanity check

₹20,000 a month for 15 years means ₹36 lakh of contributions before returns. Always calculate this base amount first; it helps distinguish what you invested from what the return assumption adds.

Monthly rate conversion

A calculator commonly converts an annual assumption to a monthly rate for periodic compounding. Different conventions can produce slightly different results, especially around contribution timing.

Step-up SIPs

If a ₹20,000 SIP rises by 10% each year, later contributions become much larger. The resulting corpus increases partly because more money was invested—not only because compounding became stronger.

Use a range, not one heroic number

Instead of planning from only 12% or 15%, compare lower, middle and higher assumptions. If a goal works only at the most optimistic assumption, the plan may be fragile.
