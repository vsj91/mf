Four levers

Current corpus, monthly contribution, time and return assumption drive the model. Of these, contribution and time are often more controllable than future return.

Inflation reality

₹1 crore in the future will not have today's purchasing power. A nominal target should eventually be connected to the real goal it is meant to fund.

Avoid return-chasing

If the target date looks too far away, raising the return assumption does not improve the real plan. Increasing contribution or extending the timeline changes something under your control.

Use milestones

Instead of focusing only on the final crore, track contribution consistency and intermediate corpus milestones. This makes the plan easier to review when income or goals change.

Example: why a step-up matters

A yearly contribution increase can materially reduce the estimated time to a target because more money is invested in later years. The improvement is not evidence that the market became safer; it comes from higher savings.

How PlanSIP keeps this transparent

Inputs, assumptions and limitations are shown rather than hidden behind a single recommendation. Historical data and estimated future returns are treated differently. A screen or score is a research aid, not a buy signal.
