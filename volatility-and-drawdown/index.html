Volatility and Drawdown: Read Risk Before Comparing Returns

A return number says where an investment ended. Volatility and drawdown help describe how difficult the journey was. Neither statistic predicts the next fall, but both can make historical comparisons more honest.

Written for: Indian mutual fund beginners   •   Last reviewed: 18 August 2026

Volatility in plain language

Volatility describes how widely returns or values have moved around their typical path. A fund that moves sharply up and down generally has higher historical volatility than a steadier one.

Drawdown in plain language

Drawdown measures decline from a previous peak. If a value rises to ₹1,20,000 and later falls to ₹90,000 before recovering, the decline from that peak is 25%.

Why maximum drawdown matters

Maximum drawdown shows the deepest observed peak-to-trough loss in the chosen history. It can help an investor ask a behavioural question: could I have stayed invested through a similar historical decline?

Same return, different journey

Two funds can end a five-year period with similar CAGR while one suffered much deeper interim falls. Looking only at CAGR would hide that difference.

Limitations

Historical volatility and drawdown depend on the data frequency and observation period. A calm past period can understate future risk; a crisis-heavy window can overstate what is typical.
