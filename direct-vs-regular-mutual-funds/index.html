Direct vs Regular Mutual Funds: What Actually Changes

Direct and Regular plans can invest in the same underlying scheme portfolio, yet their costs and distribution arrangements differ. That difference can compound over long holding periods.

Written by: Vinay S Jain, creator of PlanSIP   •   Last reviewed: 18 August 2026

Core difference

Direct plans are purchased without the distributor commission structure associated with Regular plans. Regular plans are distributed through intermediaries. Always verify the exact plan name before investing.

Why NAVs differ

Even when the underlying portfolio is the same, different recurring expenses create different NAV paths over time. A higher or lower absolute NAV is not itself the comparison; look at the same scheme, same option and the long-term effect of costs.

A simple cost illustration

If two otherwise identical return paths differ by 0.75 percentage point a year in ongoing cost, the gap can become meaningful over 10–20 years because the difference compounds. This is an illustration, not a claim about a particular scheme’s current expense ratio.

When Regular may still be used

Some investors value human distribution/service support. The decision is not simply “Direct always good, Regular always bad”; it depends on what service is being received, its value to the investor and whether conflicts/costs are understood.
