Mutual Fund Finder: Screen Funds by Goal, Time and Historical Risk

The Fund Finder starts with the investor's stated goal, time horizon and risk preference, then organizes available historical fund data. It is intended to narrow a research universe—not produce a personalized buy instruction.

Step 1: category fit before recent return

A short-term goal and a long-term wealth goal should not begin with the same category shortlist. PlanSIP first uses goal/time/risk inputs to define a more relevant search space.

Step 2: use usable history

When NAV history is available, PlanSIP can study longer-period return behaviour, volatility, drawdown, consistency and how much history exists. A new or incomplete scheme can have unavailable metrics.

Step 3: rank for research, not suitability certification

The PlanSIP score is an analytical screen. It does not incorporate every official scheme fact, your tax position, complete portfolio overlap, future manager decisions or personal financial situation.

What to verify before acting

Check the exact scheme/plan/option, investment objective, portfolio, official Riskometer, expense ratio, exit load, latest factsheet, taxation and AMC disclosures.

Example: why highest 1-year return can lose the screen

A fund with the strongest recent return can rank below another fund if the longer history shows much deeper drawdowns, higher volatility, less consistency or weaker fit with the chosen goal. This is intentional: PlanSIP is designed not to make one recent return the entire decision.

How PlanSIP keeps this transparent

Inputs, assumptions and limitations are shown rather than hidden behind a single recommendation. Historical data and estimated future returns are treated differently. A screen or score is a research aid, not a buy signal.
