Growth vs IDCW: How Mutual Fund Options Differ

Growth and IDCW are different ways a scheme can handle distributable surplus. The most important beginner insight is that an IDCW payout is not free money added on top of the fund value.

Written for: Indian mutual fund beginners   •   Last reviewed: 18 August 2026

Growth option

In a Growth option, gains remain reflected in the scheme NAV instead of being distributed as IDCW. This can make long-term compounding and return comparison more straightforward.

IDCW option

IDCW stands for Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal. A distribution can reduce the NAV by the amount and effects applicable under the scheme process. It should not be confused with bank interest or a guaranteed dividend.

A ₹10,000 mental model

If an investment worth ₹10,000 makes a distribution of ₹500, it is wrong to think the investor necessarily now has ₹10,500 of economic value. The distribution comes from the scheme and the NAV adjusts accordingly, subject to actual market movements and applicable rules.

Why PlanSIP prefers Growth for historical screens

Growth variants are generally cleaner for NAV-based long-term return comparisons because distributions do not introduce the same payout interpretation problem. PlanSIP still asks users to verify exact scheme options.
