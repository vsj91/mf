Learn

A simple investing classroom for beginners. Learn what the numbers mean before using them.

Start here

A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it in stocks, bonds, gold, silver, or other assets.

Direct lesson link

PlanSIP is designed as a study tool, not as a substitute for personal financial advice. A good investment choice depends on goal, time horizon, risk comfort, taxation, and ability to stay invested.

  • For money needed soon, stability usually matters more than high return.
  • For long-term goals, equity-oriented investments can be studied more seriously.
  • Research the asset and risk before focusing on recent returns.

SIP

Systematic Investment Plan means investing a fixed amount regularly, usually monthly.

Direct lesson link

Each monthly investment buys units based on that day's NAV. SIP reduces timing pressure, but it does not remove market risk.

  • SIP is a method of investing, not a guaranteed return product.
  • Longer horizons can provide more time to ride through market cycles.
  • A yearly SIP step-up can materially increase long-term corpus.

NAV

Net Asset Value is the per-unit value of a mutual fund.

Direct lesson link

Your fund value is approximately the number of units you own multiplied by the latest NAV.

  • Lower NAV does not automatically mean cheaper or better.
  • Higher NAV does not automatically mean expensive.
  • Percentage return is more meaningful for comparison than NAV level.

CAGR

Compound Annual Growth Rate shows the annualized growth between two points.

Direct lesson link

CAGR is useful for longer-period comparison, but it hides volatility and drawdowns along the way.

Short-term returns

1M, 3M and 6M returns show recent movement and can change quickly.

Direct lesson link

Recent performance should not be the main reason for choosing a long-term investment.

Volatility

Volatility measures how much investment values move around.

Direct lesson link

Higher volatility can mean stronger upside and sharper losses. Match volatility to your time horizon and risk tolerance.

Drawdown

Drawdown is the fall from a previous high.

Direct lesson link

Maximum drawdown is useful for understanding how painful a historical bad phase may have been.

Direct vs Regular

Direct plans avoid distributor commission. Regular plans include it.

Direct lesson link

Direct and Regular variants may hold the same portfolio but can have different expense ratios.

Growth vs IDCW

Growth keeps gains invested. IDCW may distribute income.

Direct lesson link

Growth variants are often easier to compare with NAV-based historical returns.

Debt, hybrid, equity

Debt focuses on bonds, hybrid mixes assets, and equity invests mainly in stocks.

Direct lesson link

Fund category should match the goal and timeline before you compare individual funds.

Large, mid, small cap

Company size often affects growth potential and volatility.

Direct lesson link
  • Large cap: comparatively established companies.
  • Mid cap: higher growth potential with higher risk.
  • Small cap: potentially higher volatility and longer recovery periods.

Fund Score

PlanSIP combines historical return, consistency, volatility, drawdown and available history.

Direct lesson link

The score is an educational screen, not a buy signal.

Stock basics

A stock represents ownership in a listed company.

Direct lesson link

Stock prices can move because of earnings, valuation, interest rates, sector conditions, news, market sentiment, and expectations about the future.

  • Do not judge a company only by its share price.
  • Study business quality, growth, debt, valuation and risks.
  • Analyst ratings are opinions, not guarantees.

P/E ratio

Price-to-Earnings compares a company's share price with earnings per share.

Direct lesson link

A high P/E can reflect high growth expectations, but it can also mean the stock is expensive. Compare P/E with the company's growth, industry and historical range.

Market capitalization

Market cap is approximately share price multiplied by shares outstanding.

Direct lesson link

It helps describe the market value and relative size of a listed company.

Common mistakes

Do not choose an investment only because it recently gave the highest return.

Direct lesson link
  • Chasing recent returns.
  • Ignoring valuation or risk.
  • Using equity for money needed very soon.
  • Assuming past CAGR will repeat.
  • Treating an analyst rating or PlanSIP score as a buy signal.
